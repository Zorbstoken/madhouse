# HELP WANTED — 27 open items

Everything below is real, ranked, and waiting for someone. Each item names the
smallest first step. Comment on doctrine items; build the rest.

---

## A. Naming & doctrine — the founder decides (comment, don't code)

1. **Name the transference layer.** The thing that moves Dots Pocket-to-Pocket
   has no name. Not the Exchange (that's offers/needs), not MADHOUSE (that's
   the game). *First step: propose three candidates with one-line rationales.*
2. **The verb set.** We have `gift` and `trade`. Is there a plain `send`?
   What do you call paying an NPC versus paying a player?
   *First step: list every money-movement in the game and give each one verb.*
3. **Dots vs. Zorbs at the moment of transaction.** Doctrine says "Dots you
   count; Zorbs you receive." Is the rule: I *spend* Dots, I *receive* Zorbs?
   *First step: write the one-paragraph transaction liturgy.*
4. **Orb → Dot convertibility.** Student reward points: convertible to Dots,
   or never? This is monetary policy — only the founder rules.
   *First step: argue both sides in under 200 words.*
5. **The name facing Japan.** Oliver, Xorbs, or Xiorbs? (オリバー works in
   katakana; the X strangles.) *First step: say it out loud three times and
   pick the one you'd shout across a station.*
6. **Pool control.** The oldest open question in the design: who holds the
   keys to the Pool? *First step: three governance sketches, one page each.*

## B. Launch-critical — before November 20

7. **Seeding plan.** Zero students or unions contacted. The engine grows
   weekly; the first-100 strategy doesn't exist. *First step: name 10 specific
   campuses/unions and the poster-run order. Seed, don't blast.*
8. **Walkout mechanics.** November 20 is the launch, but the walkout has no
   game expression. *First step: one Marlo quest ("Walk out on Nov 20") plus a
   pack check-in counter.*
9. **Pack primitives.** The vision is wolf packs; the build is 1:1 matching.
   *First step: a named group object (3–12 members) with a shared wish board.*
10. **Trust ladder.** Anonymous Zorbian names are perfect for play; real-world
    meetups need a graduated bridge. *First step: paper design — Zorbian name
    (play) → vouched handle (a pack member vouches) → verified presence (met
    in person, both confirm). No code until the paper holds.*
11. **Content moderation.** Wishes, bios, offers, needs are user-generated.
    *First step: one-tap report on wishes/bios/offers, routed to a human
    review queue. Automation later.*
12. **Legal review.** Minors, a game currency, walkout organizing — flagged
    for weeks, never scheduled. *First step: one qualified lawyer, one hour,
    three questions.*

## C. Game depth

13. **Quest completion loop.** Marlo pays rewards on honor today.
    *First step: report-back → acknowledgment → small reward from a visible
    capped budget. Decide who witnesses: self-report, peer confirm, photo?*
14. **Progression hook.** Nothing accumulates across days yet.
    *First step: pick one — streaks, badges, or a collection of completed
    quests — and name the level-up.*
15. **Donation enforcement.** The anti-gouge Donation exists as rules text; no
    checkout flow enforces it. A bathtub with no drain.
    *First step: enforce it at one NPC checkout.*
16. **Peer-given thanks.** The only generosity metric that isn't surveillance:
    thanks *received*, never system-computed. *First step: a thanks button on
    completed trades; count received, never derive.*
17. **Living map.** Green NPC dots, red opt-in human dots, click-for-bio.
    *First step: render the five NPCs as green dots with bio cards. Humans
    come later, coarse + opt-in + revocable, under-16 gated.*
18. **Proximity encounters.** Phones seeing nearby phones — no coordinates, no
    tracking. *First step: design the consent handshake on paper before any
    code. Per-encounter consent, never blanket.*
19. **Season one.** Launches die in December without a second act.
    *First step: one page — "the 30 days after the walkout" — with three
    beats.*

## D. Engine & scale

20. **Anti-Sybil.** One Telegram account = one 100-Dot grant, trivially
    farmable. Fine for friends; dangerous at walkout scale.
    *First step: phone-verified Telegram accounts for the welcome grant —
    invisible to honest players.*
21. **Identity recovery.** A lost Telegram account means lost Dots, no
    recourse. *First step: design recovery that doesn't create a honeypot.*
22. **iOS native path.** The Telegram Mini App covers iPhones meanwhile.
    *First step: assess the bitchat-ios fork's signing needs on a Mac.*
23. **Always-on host + HTTPS.** The engine runs local; the bot needs a home.
    *First step: one evening — host, tunnel, `configure_bot.py`, first-light
    verification (join, Pocket, trade token, shop, quests, conservation).*

## E. Lore & rules

24. **Master codex.** The Zorbs universe journey + the MADHOUSE game as one
    document: lore bible on one side, game controls on the other.
    *First step: table of contents, one page.*
25. **Professional ruleset review.** "We're going to have to go through it
    with a group of game developers." *First step: recruit two.*
26. **Participation metrics.** The vision names generosity score, participation
    level, contribution — but a system-computed generosity score is
    surveillance with a nicer name. *First step: define each as self-declared
    or peer-given only; write the red lines.*
27. **Artwork custody / the Vault.** One real artwork, real custody, one fixed
    Dot supply — phase two. *First step: the story, not the lawyers — what
    does the Vault mean to a player?*
