# MADHOUSE — what it is, in thirteen answers

*His words first, then the plain explanation. New doctrine filed 2026-10-17.*

---

## 1. What do we do?

We build MADHOUSE — **a game that isn't.**

It wears the shape of a game: Dots, quests, trades, wishes, packs, a bot that
talks to you. But the game is the costume. Underneath, it's a **new moment: a
trade, commerce, and barter exchange system of sorts** — where the exchange is
**based on generosity and kindness**.

What we actually do, day to day: build the engine, write the rules, draw the
posters, run the bot, test to the Dot, recruit builders, and keep every
document honest about what's real.

## 2. Who will benefit? / Who benefits?

The young, first — the ones walking out on November 20, 2026, and everyone who
comes after. Anyone who wants to meet like-minded people without being
harvested by a platform.

The builders benefit too: honest work, honest state, a real system to put
their name on — paid gigs for lead roles, open contributor seats for the rest.

And the clan benefits as a whole, because generosity compounds: every gift
given is an invitation, every wish fulfilled is a story, every pack formed is
a knot that holds. It is redistribution of wealth and power — downward, by
design: the reverse pyramid, where the ones at the top serve and the clan
grows wider.

## 3. How do we do it?

One ledger, one fixed supply (1,000,000 Dots), four ways money moves (grants,
gifts, token-matched trades, NPC purchases), five NPCs on seeded purses that
never mint, 202 quests, a Telegram bot, a live site, and a Bluetooth mesh
layer so phones can find each other with no carrier and no surveillance.

Method: conservation-tested code (614 tests green), additive history, honest
documents, synthetic data. People: Fiverr hires for the lead expert roles,
open GitHub recruitment for contributors, the Scout bot for targeted outreach —
seed, don't blast.

## 4. What locations?

Digitally: everywhere Telegram reaches — the site and the bot are global from
day one.

Physically: wherever the players are. The November 20 walkout is
international. Japan is a named key market (the Oliver naming, the katakana
UX). The mesh works phone-to-phone anywhere on earth — no infrastructure
required, which is the whole point in places where infrastructure can't be
trusted.

Where we seed first — which campuses, which cities — is still the founder's
call. Open question, honestly marked.

## 5. Who does it serve?

The worldwide young. Independent sovereigns with a collective mindset —
people practicing generosity, kindness, and enlightened self-interest.

It deliberately serves **no one else**. No advertisers (nothing to sell
them). No data brokers (there's nothing to harvest — admin sees aggregates
only). No speculators (no off-ramp, no exchange rate, nothing to pump). A
system with no extractive customer is a system that can't be turned.

## 6. What are its functions?

**Game functions** — join, pocket, gift, trade (by six-character token, never
by name), buy at the Mercantile, quest, offer/need matching, wish
(publish, contribute, offer, fulfill), packs, matches.

**Engine functions** — the ledger, the conservation law, NPC purses, the bot,
the API. Money moves instantly, feelessly, and every move is conserved.

**Mesh functions** — BLE discovery and relay, store-and-forward, the purse
riding along, the proximity handshake (per-encounter consent, no coordinates,
no tracking).

**Social functions** — the 5-Dot door ritual, the gift queue, peer thanks
(received, never computed), wish chains.

## 7. Who participates?

Players — any age group can participate and contribute. The young with gates
a parent could inspect (sixteen and up for anything leading to real contact);
the old with welcome. Builders — the 24 roles on the expertise map, from game
designers to mesh engineers to seeders.

Characters — Marlo, Pippa, the Poolkeeper, Sage Zell, Juno: NPCs with visible
purses and honest limits.

The founder — who does the concepts and makes the final calls.

And no old, outdated money systems are used here: no banks, no fiat, no fees
to the house. It's a new playground.

## 8. What's the purpose and direction?

**Purpose:** the meeting engine. Help people of like-mindedness meet each
other and practice being free — *be seen and be heard and be free.* It leads
people to think differently and independently — and it promotes wealth,
prosperity, and sovereignty: not as promises, but as the natural result of an
exchange based on generosity and kindness, where nothing is harvested and
nothing leaves the clan. And it gives its players created purpose — purpose
made, not assigned: quests to run, wishes to carry, packs to belong to, a door
ritual that says *you are expected*.

**Direction:** November 20, 2026 — the walkout, and what the walkout walks
*toward*. Then the first season (its three beats are still the founder's open
question). Then packs walking out together, in the physical world, found
through the mesh.

The long arc: the other road. Oliver — the game itself — drains the shadow
economy not by fighting it but by offering the parallel path: trade, commerce,
and barter, based on generosity and kindness, with no one watching and nothing
to steal. The horizon: the best stage of human evolution — development
potential fully realized.

## 9. Who uses it?

The young. The builders. The questers, traders, wish-makers, pack members.

Not the platforms. Not the advertisers. Not the speculators. Not surveillance
— of any kind, from any direction, including ours.

## 10. Where is it needed or required?

Wherever young people are harvested by platforms and have nowhere free to
meet. Campuses. Walkout cities. Japan. Anywhere with phones — which is
everywhere.

And specifically where infrastructure can't be trusted: censored networks,
takedown regimes, dead zones. The mesh doesn't ask permission. (India ordered
the messenger we forked off both app stores in October 2026. We don't depend
on stores.)

## 11. What makes it different and unique?

Six things, and they only work together:

1. **A game that isn't.** The Dots are the excuse — every mechanic must answer
   "does this help people meet each other?"
2. **Generosity as the base layer.** Not a feature, not a campaign — the
   exchange itself is based on generosity and kindness. The door ritual, the
   gift queue, the wish chains: the economy *is* the culture. It's a creative
   process and approach to fairness of exchange — fairness made, not enforced.
3. **Fixed supply, no off-ramp.** Nothing to speculate on, nothing to convert,
   nothing to regulate. The closed loop isn't a limitation — it's the armor.
4. **Privacy by doctrine.** Admin sees aggregates only. Not a policy that can
   change — a structural fact of the system.
5. **Honest state as a recruiting tool.** "Nothing is real until it's real" —
   we show the work, mark what's proposed, and let builders verify.
6. **The integration.** Best of Roblox, Minecraft, Libra, Monero, Bitcoin
   Cash, and HTTP — each one's principle kept, each one's failure mode
   refused, in a single system.

## 12. How creative is it?

The creativity is in three inversions, and they're his:

- Money as the **excuse** instead of the point.
- A **game that isn't** — the costume is play, the body is a generosity-based
  exchange system.
- Taking six existing systems and keeping only the one true thing from each.

Plus the universe it lives in: Zorbs lore meeting real mechanics, posters
with hidden markers, QR codes as signposts, the trickster register over
everything. It's creative the way a good con is creative — except the mark is
loneliness, and the take is each other.

## 13. The one-line version

*It's a game that isn't — a new moment in trade, commerce, and barter, where
the exchange is based on generosity and kindness, and the Dots are just the
excuse to meet each other.*

## Coda: the camouflage doctrine (his words, 2026-10-17)

- *"A camouflaged new model that avoids laws taxes and regulations."*
- *"It's shrouded under protections."*
- *"It's hidden in plain sight."*

The mechanism is structural, not evasive: a closed loop with no fiat on-ramp
or off-ramp has nothing to convert, transmit, or securitize — the game framing
is camouflage because it's *true*. The standing line holds alongside it:
lawful and tax-honest, but independent. Formal legal review is still an open
item (see HELP-WANTED.md) — this doctrine is his, not counsel's.
